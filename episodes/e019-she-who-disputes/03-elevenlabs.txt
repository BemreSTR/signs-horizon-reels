A woman argued with a Prophet. A chapter was named after her.

Medina. Her name was Khawla. In anger, her husband said one sentence: you are to me like my mother's back.

By their custom, those words were a trap. She was not divorced. She was not a wife. She was NOWHERE.

The reports say he had spent her youth, then left her in limbo.

She went to Muhammad, peace be upon him. At first he said: fear Allah. He is your cousin. No revelation had come yet.

She did not leave. She turned her complaint to Allah. Aisha, in the same room, could not catch every word. But Allah heard all of it.

Then came the verse: Allah has heard the woman who argues with you about her husband and complains to Allah. And Allah hears the two of you talking.

The Quran calls his words a wrong and a falsehood. His path back was atonement: free a slave, fast two months, or feed sixty poor people.

The chapter is called Al-Mujadila: She Who Disputes. Her story is reported. The verses are the Quran.

She was not a queen. She was not a scholar. She was a woman who would not stop asking. Allah kept her question.
